What is Web5? DWN + DID Explained Simply

Web5 is a way to build apps where you keep your own data and identity. Your data lives in your personal Decentralized Web Node (DWN) and your identity is a Decentralized Identifier (DID) that only you control. No company account, no central database, no middleman.

What is a DWN (Decentralized Web Node)?

Think of a DWN as your personal digital locker. On a normal app, your posts and photos sit in the company's database. In Web5, they are written to your node. Apps must ask your permission to read or write specific records, and you can revoke that access anytime. On MILAN, every member gets a real, isolated DWN the moment they sign up.

What is a DID (Decentralized Identifier)?

A DID is a self-owned identity — a unique identifier backed by cryptographic keys that you hold. An email login belongs to the company that issued it; a DID belongs to you. Nobody can suspend it, sell it, or impersonate it without your keys.

Web5 vs Web3 — what's the difference?

  • Web3 puts data and money on blockchains — tokens, gas fees, speculation.
  • Web5 skips tokens entirely: it combines the normal web (Web2) with decentralized identity and storage. No coins, no fees — just your data in your node.
