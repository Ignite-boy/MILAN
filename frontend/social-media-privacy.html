THE REAL QUESTION

What is actually being collected?

Social apps can observe much more than the content you intentionally publish. Activity, relationships, device information and interaction patterns can become part of a larger behavioral profile.

01 What you post

Photos, messages, reactions, comments and other activity.

02 Who you connect with

Relationships, frequency of interaction and network patterns.

03 How you behave

Searches, viewing patterns, clicks and engagement signals.

04 What surrounds you

Device details, permissions and contextual location information.

QUICK WINS

Privacy improvements you can make today.

Settings can reduce unnecessary exposure. They are useful, even though they do not change the underlying platform model.

01

Turn off ad personalization

Review advertising settings across your social accounts and connected services.

02

Reduce app permissions

Review location, contacts, microphone and other permissions and keep only what the application genuinely needs.

03

Audit old posts

Revisit old content and visibility settings instead of assuming yesterday's defaults still reflect your preferences.

04

Download your data

A data export can reveal how much information a platform associates with your account.

05

Limit cross-site login

Be deliberate about using a social account as an identity provider on unrelated services.

06

Choose the architecture carefully

Long-term privacy depends not only on settings, but on where identity and data live in the first place.

FAQ

Social media privacy questions

How do social media apps track you?

Platforms can collect information about posts, interactions, searches, device context, permissions and relationship patterns. The exact data varies by service.

Can privacy settings completely stop tracking?

Settings can reduce collection or exposure, but they do not necessarily change the underlying data architecture of a service.

What is the role of a DWN in privacy?

A DWN can provide a decentralized data layer in which records and access are handled according to the architecture and permission model of the application.

What is a DID?

A DID is a Decentralized Identifier used as part of decentralized identity systems.

Is decentralized social media automatically private?

No. Decentralization and privacy are related but different properties. A decentralized system still needs appropriate identity, permissions, storage and application design.

Why does architecture matter?

Architecture determines how identity, data and permissions relate to the application. Privacy controls are only one layer of that larger design.
